# Decisions — Flow redesign: build, review, and ship

## 1. The walkthrough video and annotation loop is the priority of this flow; it must work perfectly
**Decision:** Of the seven areas this flow covers, the walkthrough player and the annotate → note → fix-ticket → next-lap loop come first. The walk gives it the most attention (every scrub, pause, draw, save, jump and dead end), its grilling waypoint is the deepest, and the redesign is judged first on whether that loop works end to end without friction. Other areas are redesigned too, but none may trade against this one.
**Why:** The human's standing complaint: "the annotation flow doesn't work well and isn't very user friendly", restated at charting as "I want to get it to work perfectly". The loop is also the one the human chose to keep whole rather than split, because it cannot be judged in halves.

## 2. Ideation is mapped
**Decision:** This flow is charted into a waypoint map (ADR-0001) rather than grilled in one window: an AFK research pass over the prior designs and audit findings, three serial walk tasks that write `flow-map.md`, a confirm-the-map grilling with the human, then one grilling per design area and one for code shape / `styles.css` retirement. Spec and tickets happen in the converge session, which is also where the lap-1 slicing question is asked.
**Why:** The walk alone (success / failure / cancel burns, a review with a walkthrough, drives with and without a dev command, annotation, both triage paths, a lap 2, a real conflict, merge, shipped) is hours of agent time and dozens of screenshots — more than one context window can hold and still spec from — and seven design areas plus six prior designs to reconcile sit on top of it. Confirmed by the human on 2026-09-03.

## 3. The as-is map of review, test drive, annotation, triage and laps is `flow-map.md` §B–§F; the annotation complaint has a root cause
**Decision:** `flow-map.md` sections B (review landing), C (test drive), D (walkthrough player + annotation), E (notes triage) and F (laps) are the recorded baseline for those areas, walked live on 2026-09-04 with screenshots `v01`–`v33` under `prototypes/walk-fixtures/shots/`. The redesign treats these as facts, not hypotheses: (1) **the annotation loop breaks, it is not merely awkward** — the second stroke on a frame (or the first stroke of a second annotation) crashes the whole feature view to the error boundary, deterministically, because `startStroke` reads the pointer event's `currentTarget` inside a lazily-run React state updater; the human can save at most one single-stroke annotation per page load, and a circle-plus-arrow is impossible. (2) The player has no loading state and no error state — a 20-minute recording and a corrupt file both look like a dead card for as long as they take — and no keyboard, frame-accuracy (2 s per slider pixel at 20 min) or a way to keep the whole frame and its controls on screen at once (the sticky bar covers the frame's top). (3) Jump-to-moment moves the playhead but never brings the player into view; editing or triaging an annotated note hides its picture and timestamp. (4) F3 and F22 are closed as the research said; batch-promote appends no review ticket; a failed Iterate rolls back correctly but leaves no trace on the page. (5) From lap 2 the review-agent row and the walkthrough card vouch for lap 1's review beside a correctly lap-scoped account block; Burn counts and burns every pending ticket across laps with no lap grouping in the run view; the lap banner's past-tense copy renders before the lap session has run. The scratch feature is left in lap 2 with seven open notes for walk D.
**Why:** The human's verdict ("doesn't work well and isn't very user friendly") needed a location, and the walk found one that outranks the ergonomics list from the research: a crash on the second stroke explains a loop that feels broken far better than a thin pen or a low-on-the-page card. Recording the crash and the lap/vouching contradictions as walked facts — with the mechanism named — lets the confirm session put them to the human as baseline rather than re-deriving them, and lets the grilling waypoints (7, 8) start from "the loop must survive two strokes, show its evidence at triage and stop vouching across laps" instead of from the ergonomics alone.

## 4. The as-is map of conflict, merge and the shipped view is `flow-map.md` §G; F21/F8 are closed at the bar but the shipped record keeps living
**Decision:** `flow-map.md` section G is the recorded baseline for the conflict card, the merge dialog, the shipped body, the outcome doc, the read-only review view and the "Ask a question" Q&A terminal — walked live on 2026-09-04 by colliding the scratch repo's `main` with the feature branch, resolving through `use-resolve-conflict`, merging and shipping, with screenshots `m01`–`m25` under `prototypes/walk-fixtures/shots/`. The `DriveFailureCard` and `StopReviewDrive` branches walk C/D never reached are covered here too (§G). The redesign treats these as facts: (1) **F21 is closed** — Merge & ship opens a read-to-confirm `MergeFeatureDialog` (a green "what lands" summary + a warnings box), not a one-click merge. (2) **F8 is closed at the bar but not in the dialog** — over a standing conflict the bar's primary flips to "Resolve the merge conflict" and Merge & ship demotes to a secondary "Retry Merge & ship" with nothing disabled (decisions 2b/3), but that retry opens the *same all-green dialog that never mentions the conflict*, so a human reading only the dialog sees an all-green "what lands" over a branch that will re-conflict. (3) **The next-step bar visually collapses to one-word-per-line whenever the ConflictCard is up** — a real layout break in the state that most needs reading. (4) **The shipped record keeps living**: the lap banner still narrates lap 2 on a shipped feature; the outcome doc is written and dated (`Shipped:`) at the *pre-merge* commit and reprints the burner-digest noise verbatim; the conflict card's timestamp and the shipped hero both read `now ago`. (5) **The `ux-issues` "conflict card live in the read-only shipped view" item is confirmed**: the card at `ReviewBody.tsx:169` is gated on `conflict &&`, never on `readonly`, and its "Resolve with agent" is a live action; the read-only Test-drive card likewise gives a live "start it from the next step" instruction. (6) **Every session this surface spawns — resolve-conflict, drive-fix, Ask-a-question — boots onto Claude Code's "Yes, I trust this folder" prompt**, the same first-act friction walk D found for the lap session; and an ended Q&A conversation that captured no transcript vanishes on reload, leaving no record of what was asked. The scratch project is noted, not torn down: `greetings-pages` is left shipped as the record for the confirm/grill sessions.
**Why:** The confirm session (waypoint 5) and the merge/shipped grill (waypoint 9) need the conflict/merge/ship half as walked fact, not audit hypothesis — F8 and F21 were open findings and `ux-issues` listed the read-only conflict card as "left undone", and the walk settles all three against the live code: F21 and F8 are handled well at the *bar* level, so the redesign's work there is narrower than the findings imply (the dialog's conflict-blindness and the bar's layout break), while the shipped-record problems (a lap banner on shipped, an outcome doc dated before it shipped, a live conflict action in a history view, and a trust-prompt on every Q&A) are the surface's real open work. Naming them here — with the exact gate (`conflict &&` vs `readonly`) and the exact copy bug (`relTime` "now" + " ago") — lets waypoint 9 start from the fixes rather than re-deriving the map, and closes the three-walk baseline (decisions 3 and 4 together cover §A–§G).

## 5. The flow map is confirmed as the baseline; the human's own issues are added to it
**Decision:** `flow-map.md` §A–§G (walked 2026-09-03/04, shots `r01`–`r10`, `v01`–`v33`, `m01`–`m25`) plus `research/prior-designs.md`'s "still open" list are the confirmed baseline for this redesign, in the shape of onboarding's decision 1: the redesign is judged against these facts, and every listed dead end either gets a design decision or an explicit park. The human confirmed the map on 2026-09-04 and confirmed hitting the second-stroke crash personally — the crash is the located root of the standing complaint, with the ergonomics list behind it. The human added seven issues the walk could not have known, now part of the baseline and binding on the grill waypoints (6–9):
1. **The review page is cluttered and hard to navigate.** The important things — the walkthrough video and "Open app" — must be at the top and prominent; today the video is sixth on the page and Open app is below the fold. *(→ waypoint 7, layout; waypoint 8, player placement.)*
2. **The video must be bigger** — it should take up much more of the review page than the current 788 px card.
3. **The player needs playback-speed controls, defaulting to 1.5×** — AI browser-use walkthroughs are slow to watch at 1×. *(→ waypoint 8.)*
4. **There is far too much text on the review page** — the digest walls (burner digests, review prose, explainer paragraphs) are text most users won't read, and it reads as confusing rather than informative. Prose must be demoted to progressive disclosure; the page leads with evidence and state, not paragraphs. *(→ waypoints 6, 7, 9 — the outcome doc reprints the same walls.)*
5. **Test notes are not useful enough when the human tests the app themselves.** A self-testing human wants to paste screenshots into a note; today only a video annotation can carry a picture. Notes must accept pasted/attached images. *(→ waypoint 8; the upload route keyed by note id already exists.)*
6. **"Open app" should open in an integrated panel with annotation tools like the video's** — the human selects an area of the live app that needs fixing and a screenshot is attached to the note automatically. *(→ waypoint 7 with waypoint 8's tools; see decision 8 for the scope call this needs — `test-drive-improvements` locked "no embedded browser", which this revisits.)*
7. **The review evidence shown as current is stale by design.** The review agent reviews the code and records the video, then mints fix tickets; those tickets burn and change the app, but no re-review follows — so the video, digest text, defects and notes the page presents as the feature's current state describe a build that no longer exists. The notes/defects surfaces compound this and read as "confusing and outdated". Every piece of review evidence must be presented with its moment (what it reviewed, what has landed since), and the grills must decide how the page distinguishes fresh evidence from superseded evidence. *(→ waypoints 6, 7, 8; whether a re-review pass runs after fix waves is a workflow question — see the scope decision to come.)*
**Why:** The confirm session exists so the human can add what the walk missed, and they did: items 1–4 sharpen the "most crowded surface" question into a concrete ordering (video and Open app first, text last), items 5–6 extend the annotation loop beyond the video onto the live app and self-taken screenshots, and item 7 names a truth problem — the page vouches with stale evidence — that underlies the walk's cross-lap vouching finding but is broader than it. Recording them here, routed to their waypoints, is what carries the human's words to the other waypoint agents.

## 6. The visual bar and spacing rhythm; what a returning human sees first on the review page
**Decision:** This flow is judged by the two prior flows' standards, adopted verbatim: onboarding decision 1's visual bar (deliberate vertical rhythm, clear type hierarchy kicker → heading → one-line lead → control, readable secondary text, no cards that are mostly empty space) and project-chat decision 9's spacing rhythm (Tailwind 4 px scale: 8 px inside a control group, 16 px between fields, 24 px between sections/cards, 32 px header-to-body; nothing closer than 8 px or farther than 32 px except at a section boundary). On top of that, the review page — the most crowded surface in the app — gets an ordering rule: **evidence first, prose last.** A returning human sees, in order: the walkthrough video (big — much more of the page than today's 788 px card) and the Open app affordance at the top and prominent; then current state (checks, open defects/notes, lap position); prose (digests, explainers, review text) is demoted to progressive disclosure at the bottom or behind it. The digest wall never again opens the page.
**Why:** Confirmed by the human 2026-09-04: "I want the user to have the important things (video, open app, etc.) at the top and more prominent … there's also a lot of text in the review page which most users won't read, it's kind of confusing." The rhythm is a rule, not taste, so seven areas designed across several grill sessions land looking like one flow.

## 7. Self-testing gets the video's annotate-and-attach loop on the live app; pasted screenshots are the floor
**Decision:** Two locked intents. (a) **Notes accept pasted/attached images** unconditionally — a self-testing human pastes a screenshot into a note and it rides the existing note-screenshot pipeline (upload route keyed by note id, thumbnail, promotion attachment) exactly as a video annotation's PNG does. (b) **"Open app" opens in an integrated panel on the review page with the annotation tools the video has** — select an area of the live app, a screenshot with the selection is attached to a note. This deliberately reverses `test-drive-improvements`' "no embedded browser": that decision predates the human's self-testing experience and is superseded for the review surface. Because the dev server is cross-origin (an iframe displays but its pixels cannot be read client-side), a prototype waypoint (charted from this session) must prove the capture mechanism — proxying the dev server through runcastle's origin vs. a server-side capture — before waypoint 7 commits to a mechanism; if the prototype has not resolved when waypoint 7 runs, it designs the panel UI mechanism-agnostic and leaves the capture path to the prototype's verdict. Pasted screenshots (a) are the floor that works regardless.
**Why:** The human: "The test notes don't feel very useful … he would want to paste screenshots for example. I think it would make sense that 'Open App' would open in an integrated panel with tools like those for video annotation." The annotation loop is this flow's priority (decision 1), and extending its evidence-capture to the human's own testing is the same loop, not a new one.

## 8. Re-review is in scope: after fix waves land, the review evidence refreshes, and the UI always shows the latest
**Decision:** The staleness problem is fixed at both levels, here. (a) **Workflow: a re-review pass runs after a review's fix tickets land**, so the video, digest, defect list and checks describe the build as it now is — the brief's "reflect, don't change the workflows" fence is lifted for this one change by the human's explicit instruction. (b) **The evidence system keys on latest:** the video shown is always the latest recording, the defect/finding surfaces reflect the latest review pass, and every piece of evidence is stamped with its moment (what it reviewed, what has landed since); superseded evidence is visually demoted or archived, never presented as current. This subsumes the cross-lap vouching fix (research still-open 1: lap-scoped `reviewOutcome`/`reviewChecks`/`reviewWalkthroughUrl`) — the rule is one generation stronger: not just "this lap's review", but "the latest review of the current build". The design of the pass (trigger, what re-records, cost/opt-out, interplay with laps and with `review-findings-are-fixed-in-run`'s in-run fixing) is grilled in a dedicated waypoint charted from this session, after the review-landing and annotation grills.
**Why:** The human, on being offered the park: "I want the re-review to be done here. Make sure that the video, defect, etc. system works with this (the latest video should be the one shown, etc.)." The presentation fix alone would label evidence honestly but leave it stale; the human wants it fresh.

## 9. Scope: what of the research's "still open" list is in, what is parked
**Decision:** **In scope:** correctness items 1–5 (cross-lap review pick — now subsumed by decision 8; the orphaned fix ticket that vanishes its defect; the never-retired conflict state on shipped features; batch-promote appending the review ticket `ux-issues` dec. 9 requires — taken literally, every lap's batch gets one; `readonly` honoured by all ReviewBody cards); the entire annotation-ergonomics list 7–16 (the priority area, now including the second-stroke crash); and every dead end in `flow-map.md` §A–§G — each gets a design decision or an explicit park in its grill. Tidiness items that live in this flow's files (17–20, 22–28: hand-spelled URLs, dead code, unstable findings order, phase-gated polling, per-lane digests, lap grouping of run lanes, SPEC §15.6 amendments, the live-drive explainer) ride waypoint 10's code-shape decisions and this flow's tickets. **Parked:** the burner-docs wording items (6: mounted-mode `info/exclude` overstatement; 21: undocumented clone-side re-exclude) — docs amendments to the burner, not this surface; orphan-PNG cleanup and conflict-resolver attachments (stay parked as before); the empty review-drive database (research §1.4.12 — drive tooling, not this flow); and the Windows long-path/sandcastle mount bug (walk A dead end 1) — the *UI* must surface a readable error for a launch that dies in one second (waypoint 6's grill), but the sandcastle path-rewrite fix itself is its own fix outside this flow.
**Why:** The correctness items are on this flow's path and the brief calls server bugs there fair game; the ergonomics list is the stated priority; the tidiness items are in files this flow rewrites anyway, so skipping them would preserve debt inside freshly-touched code. The parked items are either other subsystems' docs/tooling or infra whose fix would swallow the flow.

## 10. Run view: lanes are the spine; the transcript is per-lane disclosure; lanes get a real visual language
**Decision:** RunBody is rebuilt lanes-first. A returning human sees, top to bottom: a run header (progress, elapsed, the run-level control), then the ticket lanes as the page's spine. The shared Agent/Events tab pane pinned to one ticket is gone — each lane expands in place to show its own agent transcript and events, and while the container boots the expanded lane shows the Events-tab boot narrative (container create, cache sync) instead of "waiting for the agent's first output…" (kills walk dead end 5). The lanes themselves are designed, not listed: each lane carries a **runtime icon + model badge** (Claude vs Codex — runtime is a declared property of the model in the config roster, and tickets can carry per-ticket model overrides, so lanes can genuinely differ within a run), status-driven color and motion on the foundation's tokens — a live animation while `burning` (not a static chip), an animated settle into done/failed, elapsed timer while burning, short-sha chip when done. Motion is subtle and state-driven, within decision 6's visual bar and spacing rhythm.
**Why:** The walk showed the transcript is the least trustworthy element on the page (blank for the first 15–20 s of every ticket, raw tool spam, a confident `<promise>COMPLETE</promise>` over a failed lane) while the lanes carry the actual state and controls — so decision 6's "evidence and state first, prose last" puts lanes on top. The human added the visual ask directly: "improve how the lanes look… nicer visuals and animations, maybe icons for what type of model is running (claude or codex)."

## 11. A failed run gets two honest exits: per-lane Waive, and "Continue to review" once every lane is terminal
**Decision:** (a) A failed lane offers **Waive** beside Retry / Retry fresh: it cancels the ticket through the existing cancel machinery, the lane leaves the red failure state and reads as deliberately set aside, and the waived ticket is carried into review as explicitly-unfinished work (surfaced in the review landing's account — waypoint 7 designs where; the verb and its lane state are decided here). (b) When every lane is terminal and at least one ticket is done, the next-step bar offers **"Continue to review"** as a secondary beside the primary "Resume burn" — the honest partial-completion exit matching what gate G4 already believes ("every ticket reached a terminal state"). Continuing hides nothing: failed and waived tickets remain visible open work at review.
**Why:** Walk dead end 2 — a permanently-failing ticket loops forever with no exit but retry, while the G4 panel says "Ready to advance" and the bar says "Resume the burn"; the only escape is the gate's generic "Override with reason…", which buries the common "this one ticket is hopeless, the rest is fine" case behind a scary hatch. Confirmed by the human 2026-09-04.

## 12. Cancel run confirms; a deliberate stop is `stopped`, not `failed`; Stop ticket stays one click
**Decision:** (a) **Cancel run gets a confirm dialog** on the foundation's dialog primitive (never native `confirm()`), stating the blast radius: it stops N burning agents, finished work is kept, the burn can resume later. Retry fresh's native `confirm()` moves to the same primitive. (b) **`stopped` becomes a visually distinct lane state** — neutral/amber, never the red failure chip — covering both "stopped by user" and "orphaned — the run ended (cancelled)"; red is reserved for real failures. Run summaries speak the same language ("1 done · 1 failed · 2 stopped", never counting stops as failures). Stopped lanes keep Retry / Retry fresh. The state can derive from the already-recorded cause strings; no schema change is required unless implementation finds the strings unreliable. (c) **Stop ticket stays one click** — it is scoped to one agent and is the control reached for when an agent is visibly going wrong; friction there costs the most at the moment speed matters.
**Why:** Walk dead ends 3 and 4: cancel-run (kills every agent) was one unconfirmed click while the strictly less destructive Retry fresh confirmed, and a deliberate human stop was indistinguishable at a glance from a crash. Confirmed by the human 2026-09-04, including the Stop-ticket exception.

## 13. Transcript hygiene: protocol markers become UI, sandbox paths become repo-relative, failure verdicts explain themselves; Events demotes to a collapsed run timeline
**Decision:** All renderer-level, no workflow changes. (a) **Protocol tokens are swallowed and rendered as UI** — `<promise>COMPLETE</promise>` becomes a small "agent reported complete" marker at the transcript's end, and the same treatment covers any other protocol tokens the burner prompt defines. (b) **Sandbox-internal paths are rewritten repo-relative** in tool lines (`/home/agent/cache/slots/1/repo/src/App.tsx` → `src/App.tsx`). (c) **A failed lane's expanded view opens with a structured verdict strip** above the transcript: what the burner checked and what it found (e.g. "The burner verifies commits landed on the ticket branch — none did"), so a transcript ending in confident completion is framed by the verdict rather than contradicting the lane unexplained; the lane's collapsed summary keeps the short form. (d) **The Events tab demotes to a collapsed "Run timeline" section under the lanes** (run start, docs digest, per-ticket landmarks, run summary) — the debugging record, not a co-equal surface; per-ticket event detail lives in each lane's expansion per decision 10.
**Why:** Walk dead ends 6 and 7 — the burner's wire protocol leaked as the agent's last words, tool lines spoke in container paths meaningless to the human, and `failed: agent made no commits` sat under a happy transcript with nothing connecting the two accounts. Confirmed by the human 2026-09-04.

## 14. The run view narrates the review agent's in-run fixing; the header counts honestly; lanes group by lap
**Decision:** All presentation of events the workflow already emits — no workflow change. (a) **The review ticket's lane is visibly a different kind** — a "review" badge/icon beside decision 10's runtime icon, because its output is findings, not commits. (b) **Fix tickets minted mid-run arrive as a visually grouped "review fixes" wave** under the review lane, each badged with the defect it fixes and linked to the finding, arriving with decision 10's motion so the human sees the run grow instead of discovering unlabelled extra lanes. (c) **The run header recounts honestly**: "Burning 3 tickets · +2 fixes from review — 2 done", always stating done/failed/stopped; during a solo per-ticket retry it speaks about that retry ("Retrying #4"), never whole-lap numbers (closes walk dead end 8). (d) **Lanes group by lap with a lap divider** when the ledger spans laps (Burn burns every pending ticket across laps), matching the ledger's `LapSections`, so a lap-2 burn never mixes lap-1 leftovers in unlabelled.
**Why:** `review-findings-are-fixed-in-run` folds minted fix tickets into the same run, but the walk found the lanes just appear among the others, the bar's total silently mutates, and nothing marks the review lane as a different kind of work; the run view is where in-run fixing happens, so it must narrate it. (Rendering a defect as "being fixed" on the review page is waypoint 7's.) Confirmed by the human 2026-09-04.

## 15. Success settles before it advances; runs become navigable history
**Decision:** (a) **The auto-advance to review stays — no click added** — but success gets a landing: the lanes play decision 10's done-settle, a brief all-green beat, then the transition to review; a run watched to success is seen to succeed before the page changes. (b) **The status bar's "N runs" counter becomes real navigation** to a read-only run record: the same lanes-as-spine layout in terminal form, with the run timeline and per-ticket digests, and each lane's transcript when still in server memory (today's honest "transcripts are held in server memory for the current burn" note when it is not). No new persistence — this renders what the events table already keeps.
**Why:** The walk: "the run view is gone the moment the run succeeds — there is no terminal success run state to stand on", and dead end 9 — only the latest run renders and the runs counter goes nowhere. A terminal success state with a "Go to review" button was rejected as one extra click on the happy path of every run. Confirmed by the human 2026-09-04.

## 16. Burn copy sets a data-informed time expectation; zero-ticket states keep their honest behavior; a launch death gets a "launch failed" treatment
**Decision:** (a) **F25.1's fix stands**: both zero-ticket states (tickets phase and implementation-with-empty-ledger) keep their honest WAITING behavior, restyled onto the foundation's primitives. (b) **F12's other half lands in the bar**: the pre-burn copy states a time expectation, data-informed rather than hardcoded — the median of this project's past ticket durations when history exists ("tickets have been taking ~2 min each"), a generic "typically a few minutes per ticket" before any history. (c) **A lane that fails before first agent output gets a distinct "launch failed" treatment** — visually different from an agent failure: decision 13's verdict strip explains the sandbox never started, the raw `git`/`docker` error sits behind a disclosure, and a hint line appears when the error matches a known cause (e.g. long Windows paths). Retry stays available. The sandcastle path-rewrite fix itself remains parked per decision 9.
**Why:** These are the last of walk §A's findings owed a decision: F25.1 needed only reaffirmation, F12's rail copy was fixed but the bar still set no duration expectation (and a hardcoded number would rot — the project's own history is the honest source), and dead end 1 showed a run dying in ~1 s with a raw mount error and no framing. Confirmed by the human 2026-09-04.
