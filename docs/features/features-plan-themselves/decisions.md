# Features plan themselves — decisions

## 1. The planner grills itself in one turn and its own recommendations are the answers
**Decision:** A self-planning session walks the design tree by itself, in a single turn. It asks its design questions as it would ask the human, each with a recommended answer, then accepts its own recommendations, records each as an assumed decision, and goes straight on to what those answers opened. No second agent answers the questions and nothing replies to the session between rounds.
**Why:** The human proposed a cheap model that accepts every recommendation. A reply that is always "yes" carries no information, so nothing needs to send it: the instruction "accept your recommendations and continue" does the same job from inside the turn. Four hand runs on a toy feature showed it: the run with no replies at all finished its plan cleanly and as well as the runs that were sent a reply every round.

## 2. Questions are asked a whole frontier at a time
**Decision:** A self-planning session works in rounds. A round is every open question that can be answered now because it does not depend on an unanswered one. Once the round is decided, the next round is the questions those answers opened, and so on until a round is empty. This applies only when the session plans by itself: a grill with the human stays one question at a time.
**Why:** The human asked for it. One question per turn exists to spare a human reader, and there is no reader here. The rounds leave the questions and their alternatives on the record, so the human can see what was considered. The hand runs closed their trees in three to five rounds.

## 3. Nothing drives a loop in lap 1
**Decision:** Lap 1 has no `/goal`, no reply from a Stop hook and no other mechanism that sends a self-planning session back to work. The session finishes because its skill text tells it to. A run that ends before the plan is finished leaves the feature at planning with whatever it wrote; the human opens the chat, which resumes the same conversation, and carries on as in an ordinary grill. A driver is added in a later lap only if real sessions are seen stopping before the plan is done; the known fix is runcastle answering the turn-end hook it already receives.
**Why:** The driver was the only part that failed in the hand runs. `/goal`'s checker never agreed the plan was finished and ignored the turn limit written into its condition, and a Stop-hook loop ran until it was stopped by hand. Both failed by spending tokens. The run with no driver finished by itself, and its failure mode is a run that ends early, which spends nothing further. The project session's worry that a planner alone cuts corners did not appear: every run asked far more questions than a human grill does, not fewer.

## 4. Exactly one challenger pass
**Decision:** When the planner's own rounds are empty, it runs one challenger: a subagent given only the brief and the decisions, asked for design branches that were never asked and for decisions it would have made differently. The branches it names are asked as one more round and decided, with anything they open. A decision it disagrees with is marked as challenged, with the challenger's reason, and the planner's answer stands. There is no second pass and no requirement that a pass come back empty.
**Why:** In the hand runs the first pass found real gaps the planner had skipped. A rule that the pass must come back empty never held: a fresh agent asked what was missed always finds something, and one run reached its tenth challenger with the decision list more than tripled and the later findings thin. One pass is a second look, not a proof that the tree is complete; the human looking at the preview is the check after that.

## 5. Thin lap 1: the human chooses self-planning with a click, per feature
**Decision:** Lap 1 is the self-planning section of the ideate skill, the headless launch that runs it (decision 9), and a choice wherever a feature's planning session is first opened: plan it yourself, or grill me. That is the Start click on a draft and the first Chat click on a started feature with nothing planned yet. Deferred to later laps: planning sessions that start with nobody on the page (so a cut made by the project session plans itself), what dependents do, and a loop driver.
**Why:** The one real unknown is whether a plan the session makes alone is good enough to approve by looking, measured by how many of its decisions the human would overturn. Lap 1 puts that in front of the human for one click per feature. Starting a whole cut unattended multiplies the token spend on plans whose quality has not been seen yet, so it waits for the answer.

## 6. A depth rule keeps the decision list readable
**Decision:** A self-planning session records a decision only if it changes what the feature is or does for its users, or if the human might reasonably overturn it. Anything an engineer would settle while building, such as wording, internal formats and test coverage, is not asked as a question and goes into the spec as detail.
**Why:** With nobody's patience limiting it, the planner made 38 and 49 decisions for a toy feature in the two hand runs without this rule, against a median of 8 in human grills, and by its third round it was deciding tab titles. A plan with that many decisions cannot be approved by looking. With one sentence of this rule the planner made 13 and 25 in the two runs that had it. That is still more than a human grill, which is why the check-first list (decision 8) exists.

## 7. Every self-made decision is marked assumed and names its alternative
**Decision:** A decision the session made without the human is marked as assumed in `decisions.md` and carries the main alternative it was chosen over. When the human confirms or changes a decision in the feature's chat afterwards, it loses the mark and reads as any other decision.
**Why:** The record has to show who decided. With the alternative written beside each decision, overturning one costs the human a sentence in the chat, and the session does not have to reconstruct what the other option was.

## 8. At most five decisions to check first
**Decision:** The planner picks at most five decisions as the ones to check first: guesses about the human's intent that the brief did not settle, decisions that force several others, and decisions the challenger disagreed with. The preview's decisions part opens with these, each showing its alternative and why it is uncertain; the remaining decisions follow as they do in any preview.
**Why:** The aim is that the human can approve a plan by reading the show and five decisions. In the hand runs the planner's own least-sure list was three, and it included the one decision that forced six others; the challenger disagreed with seven, mostly over wording. Fewer than five risks hiding a real one, and more starts to read like the grill again.

## 9. Self-planning runs headless, and the chat resumes the same conversation
**Decision:** A self-planning session runs with no terminal, to completion, on whichever CLI the feature's model belongs to: Claude Code's non-interactive mode or Codex's `exec`. It is the same planning session as a grill in every other respect: same briefing, same skill pack, same guards, and the same conversation record. When the human wants to change the plan they open the feature's chat, which resumes that conversation in the ordinary interactive terminal, and from there it is the grill they know. Both CLIs are in lap 1. A tool the run is refused is never a wait: the planner decides on what it knows and treats that decision as a candidate to check first.
**Why:** The human proposed it. A headless run has no prompt to show, so it cannot sit on a permission request with nobody there, which a terminal session can do while still looking busy. It also ends: the process exits when the plan is done, so "finished" and "stopped early" are facts the server sees, and no idle terminal is left open per feature. Starting plans with nobody on the page, deferred to a later lap, then needs no terminal either. The cost is that the human cannot watch the agent's turns while it plans, and stopping it means ending the run, not typing into it.
**Evidence (hand runs on a toy feature):** Claude Code and Codex 0.155.1 each planned the feature headless in one turn and exited cleanly, and each finished session was resumed by its id with full context and correctly reworked one overturned decision. Codex's headless mode never asks for approval by default. Not yet tried: resuming a headless session into the interactive terminal (both resumes were headless), and a Codex run inside the session home runcastle generates. Codex's planner did not recognise its agent-spawning tool from the word "subagent" and challenged its own plan instead, though it has the tool, so the challenger instruction has to name the tool each CLI's way.

## 10. A self-planned feature goes all the way to Burn without waiting
**Decision:** A self-planning session does not stop at the preview for confirmation. It draws the preview, writes the spec and emits the tickets in the same run, and the feature is left ready to burn. The human approves by looking and clicking Burn, or opens the chat to change the plan, which amends the decisions, redraws the preview and reconciles the spec and tickets as any later change does.
**Why:** Settled in the project session: the feature stops at Burn, and the two human clicks stay. Waiting at the preview would put the human back in the middle of planning, which is the wait this feature removes. The cost is that a spec and tickets written on an assumption are rewritten when the human overturns it.

## 11. The page says the feature is planning itself, and offers Stop
**Decision:** While a self-planning run is going, the feature's next-step row says the feature is planning itself and offers one control, Stop. The chat is not offered during the run, because a feature has one session at a time. The preview and the tickets appear on the page as the run writes them. When the run finishes, the page is the ordinary planning page with the preview, the tickets and Burn, and nothing added. If the run ends before the plan is complete, or the human presses Stop, the page says planning stopped before the plan was complete and offers the chat, which resumes what the run had done. The feature list needs nothing new: it already shows a feature whose session is mid-turn as working.
**Why:** The human is told the three things they need and no more: it is working, it is done, or it stopped short. A finished self-made plan is read through the preview, which already opens with the decisions to check first, so the page does not repeat it.

## 12. Start still means a grill; "Plan it yourself" sits beside it
**Decision:** Wherever a feature's planning session is first opened, the existing action keeps its meaning: it opens the grill with the human. Self-planning is a second option beside it. The default flips only after the human's test drives show self-made plans hold up.
**Why:** Runcastle is published and other people use Start. Changing what the main button does, to a mode whose plans have not been judged yet, would spend their tokens on something they did not ask for.
