Runcastle cleans up after itself

Every container, image, build cache and folder runcastle makes is removed once nothing needs it, so runcastle never fills a disk. Nothing new to look at: it just stops leaking.

Catching what slipped through

Each runcastle install marks everything it makes with its own data folder 5. A sweep at start-up, when a test drive stops, and every hour 6 removes anything marked with this install that nothing owns, and anything whose data folder is gone (a finished drive's containers). It never touches another live install's things, so a drive can no longer remove the real install's workers.

What you see

No new screen or setting. When a sweep removes something, one line appears on the project timeline 11:

09:14Removed 3 leftover containers and 1 old image (11.2 GB)

Who builds what

  • Cleanup engine (on its own, Sonnet): ownership marks, the lifecycle changes in the worker service, the sweep and its timeline line, with tests against the fake container engine.
  • Builds and captures (on its own, Sonnet): runcastle-only image builder, removing replaced images, cache-volume temp files, review capture expiry, the drive stop hook.
  • Me (the lead): the hand-off between the two, one check on real Docker (land a worker, kill the server mid-setup, stop a drive, then confirm nothing is left), then a review round.

What is left out

Later

  • Smaller worker sandboxes: make each worker's ~4 GB a fraction of that (parked as a backlog feature) 12

Not this feature

  • A disk-usage screen, a setting, or a low-disk warning
  • Anything runcastle did not create: your own images, containers, caches, ~/.npm, ~/.bun
  • Deleting feature branches after merge
  • Anything holding work not yet on a branch, such as a dirty worktree

Under the hood

Marks. Every run, build and volume create adds labels: runcastle.owner, runcastle.data-dir (the ownership key), runcastle.worker or runcastle.project. Older containers are recognised by their origin mount (workers) or their runcastle image (retired burn engine). Every remove, stop and exec checks ownership first.

Worker service. The container name is saved right after run, before clone and install, so a setup failure can remove it. Land keeps the conversation then removes container, origin copy and branch, as drop does. The start-up sweep keeps conversations before removing. Shutdown stops running containers. Messaging a landed worker is refused.

Sweep. One serialised, never-throwing function replaces the start-up stopLeftoverWorkerSandboxes; runs at boot (not on a copied database), hourly, and from the drive stop hook. Emits cleanup.swept per project with counts and bytes, cleanup.failed on a removal error.

Images. Docker builds in a runcastle-only buildx builder (--load), removed after; Podman uses --layers=false. A replaced image is removed when unused.

Volumes and folders. Volume temp files older than a day are deleted at worker start. Volumes and images of removed projects go. Review capture folders map to their feature and expire 30 days after it closes.

Tests. Main seam: the worker service with the extended fake engine (labels plus a fake container listing). New seam: the sweep fed every label case, including the copied-database hazard. Plus argv tests and one real-Docker spike run.
