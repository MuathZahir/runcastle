# Decisions — review-arrival-is-legible

## 1. Defect boundary moves to "the human's problem is actually solved"
**Decision:** Redefine `defect` in the review agent's prompt (`packages/skills/burner/review-ticket.md`): a finding is a defect when the human's problem is not actually solved — even if every acceptance criterion passes. The DLQ case (fix landed, correct at the seam, but the endpoint never calls it, so the operator sees no change) is the canonical example: that is a defect, mints a fix ticket in-run, and appears in open work. Prompt-only change; no schema, cap, or pipeline change — `FindingKind` stays `defect | observation`.
**Why:** Evidence from a real project (2026-09-08 screenshot): the "unsure → observation" rule plus an AC-anchored defect definition demoted exactly the finding the human most needed to see, to a barely-visible line among 10 observations. Fixing classification at the source beats adding a third kind (new machinery, new bucket to learn) or asking the UI to rank prose by smuggled severity.

## 2. Residual observations are noise and get demoted hard
**Decision:** With the defect boundary redrawn (decision 1), whatever remains in the observation bucket is by construction inert (environment quirks, drift trivia). The review page demotes them aggressively — they do not occupy arrival real estate. Exact rendering (fully hidden vs. collapsed count) settles with the layout pass; recording and digest-feeding machinery is untouched.
**Why:** The human's complaint that observations "add nothing" was only safe to honor once the important-signal-misfiled-as-observation problem was fixed upstream. After decision 1, hiding them costs nothing.

## 3. The bar states its reason as an always-visible count
**Decision:** Keep the one-door priority ladder (conflict → drive → open work → pending fixes → merge), but the bar's permanent one-liner is the open-work count, not guidance-gated prose: "2 defects · 1 note open" with Iterate primary and Merge & ship as ghost secondary; "Nothing open" with Merge & ship primary when clear. The vague "Answer what is still open" title and the guidance-flag-gated explanation paragraph go away.
**Why:** The human stared at Iterate-as-primary unable to see why (2026-09-07 screenshots). The logic was right; it just never showed its input. The button follows the count and the count is right there — learnable in one exposure. Making Merge & ship always primary was rejected: the default action would be the one the system advises against.

## 4. One-terminal refusals become "End session & <verb>" compounds — everywhere
**Decision:** No surface renders a disabled button because of the one-terminal gate. Any action blocked by a live session is relabeled "End session & <verb>" (e.g. "End session & start lap 2") and, on click, awaits `endSession` then proceeds — the compound already proven by the conflict flow's "End session & resolve" (`use-resolve-conflict.ts`). No confirmation dialog: the label is the consent. This applies as a blanket rule to every button the gate currently disables (the triage carry exit, the bar's Iterate/start-lap action, and any other `ONE_TERMINAL_*` refusal found during implementation), not just the review page.
**Why:** The human clicked Iterate and dead-ended on a disabled button with "end the live session first" and no way to do it ("why doesn't it close it automatically???"). Ending a stale talk session is near-zero risk — it marks the row ended synchronously and committed docs survive; a confirm dialog would tax every lap transition to guard a case that costs nothing. Generalized on the human's instruction (2026-09-08).

## 5. Review renders no terminal — live sessions demote to a one-line alert
**Decision:** Drop the SessionPanel band from ReviewBody. A live session (any kind) shows as one slim line in the alerts band — "Ideation session still live — Open · End" — where Open jumps to that phase's view and End ends it. An ended session renders nothing on review. The readonly omission is fixed in passing (no live controls in history views).
**Why:** The panel was a uniformity fallthrough, rendered unconditionally as an undocumented first band with no phase/kind filter — producing a live ideation terminal above the fold on the review page (2026-09-07 screenshot), contradicting the band contract that review opens on the evidence stage. With decision 4, stale sessions mostly self-resolve at Iterate; the alert line is honest state, not a workspace.

## 6. The evidence stage mounts only when it has evidence
**Decision:** No walkthrough and no live drive → no stage: the 16:9 empty box and its "No walkthrough yet —" placeholder are gone, and the page opens on state and open work. The stage mounts only for a recording or a live drive. The empty box's one real payload — the entry point to take your own test drive — becomes a compact "Test drive" control in the status area. (ShippedBody already proves the omit-the-stage pattern.)
**Why:** A bordered aspect-video card containing one sentence was the largest element on arrival while carrying zero information. The human's position — no walkthrough, no panel — holds once the drive entry point is rehomed.

## 7. The lap session opens with the carried work stated, not pointed at
**Decision:** Four-part fix to the carry channel: (a) the lap kickoff line and injected system prompt name the carried work — "3 notes carried and 1 defect open from lap 1's review — address them" — counts and instruction, no "may not exist yet" hedging; (b) get_feature_context stops withholding test-notes.md whenever carried-undone notes exist (the stored withheld reason "already triaged into this lap's tickets" is false on the carry exit); (c) open defects travel too — their structured rows (title, location, detail, repro step) are included in the new session's context, closing the today-zero-channels gap; (d) all pointers key on note status (carried, not done, any lap), never on a lap-numbered section, so a note lap N+1 skipped stays visible at lap N+2. Server/prompt plumbing only; the lifecycle rule that carrying removes notes from open work (prior decision 27b) is untouched.
**Why:** The human observed lap sessions arriving ignorant of the notes that motivated them. The trace confirmed it: prior spec said "the lap session opens with those notes," shipped as a distrusted pointer to a withheld file, with defects carried through nothing and skipped notes vanishing behind wrong-lap section pointers.

## 8. The arrival layout: six bands, one disclosure, a one-liner digest
**Decision:** ReviewBody's arrival order, top to bottom: (1) alerts only when real — conflict, or the live-session line; (2) one state line — compact status chips, the unverified-checks caveat folded in as a chip when it applies, and the "Test drive" control beside them; (3) a one-line lap account ("Lap 1: <what landed> · 2 defects found, 1 fixed in-run") — the review agent's prompt changes to ask for this one-liner and to stop promising the full digest renders first; (4) the attention list (defects + notes), the visual center; (5) the note composer; (6) a single collapsed "Full account" disclosure holding the full digest prose, carried/handled, and observations — observations render only here, not even a count on arrival (settles decision 2's open detail). Killed outright: the test-drive lead sentence and explainer paragraph, the bar's guidance paragraph, the placeholder box, the terminal band. Validated by the prototype at docs/features/review-arrival-is-legible/prototypes/review-arrival-prototype.html (four states: open work, stale session, walkthrough, all clear); human approved 2026-09-08.
**Why:** The page must lead with state and one obvious action; the shipped page led with a terminal, an empty box, and prose. The digest's claimed top billing (prompt says "first thing they read") lost to the wall-of-text disease this feature cures — the one-liner keeps the account visible, the prose stays reachable.
