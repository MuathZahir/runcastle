One number for how many build at once

One setting says how many workers and reviews run at the same time, with a default taken from the machine's memory. A line in the sidebar shows who is working and who waits, in order.

The show

Before and after, on this 11 GB machine

Today

  • 1 worker at a time, because the default counts CPU cores.
  • The setting hides on the Sandboxes page, which is going away.
  • A waiting worker shows only a clock. Nothing says how many are ahead.

After

  • 2 at a time by default: one per 4 GB of memory, between 2 and 8 4. You can set up to 16 6.
  • The number sits in Settings → General 7.
  • The sidebar shows who works and who waits, in order 8.

Who takes a place

Workers and reviews

Take a place while mid-turn 5. Past the number, they wait, first come first served across every project 9.

Leads, orchestrator, epics

Never wait 2.

Waiting on you, idle

Hold no place. The next in line starts.

Usage limit hit

Keeps its spot but is skipped, and says "held: <why>" until the limit lifts.

The real sidebar with sample workers and projects. A foot line ("2 of 2 working · 3 waiting") opens the full line-up: those working, then those waiting in order. A waiting worker's row says "waiting · 2nd in line".

Under the hood
Default

A pure resolver: total memory ÷ 4 GB, clamped to 2–8, replaces the CPU-count one. Config load reads the machine's total memory. The cap rises to 16. The config key and env var keep their names, so set values survive.

Slots

The in-memory limiter learns holder ids and exposes a snapshot: who holds a slot, who waits, in what order, and which are held. Admission rules are unchanged.

Queue read

A new machine-wide worker.queue query: limit, working[], waiting[], each with feature and project. Sidebar worker rows gain queuePlace.

Live updates

A worker.queue_moved event on every admit, release or resize, so places update at once.

Checks

Unit tests on the resolver and the slot snapshot, a router test across two projects, and tier-1 markup tests on the queue line and the setting.
